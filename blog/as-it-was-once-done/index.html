As it was once done

January 19, 2023

A journey inside the workshop of a wood craftsman who recently passed away. The laboratory is looked after by his son, who tries to keep it alive.

These first shots represent immersion in the environment, the first impact one has with the laboratory. Old, dusty, lived.

Chisels, chisels and chisels. The craftsman was a carver, and as you can see, he liked chisels. They were everywhere, really everywhere. But always all grouped together, there was chaos, but an orderly chaos.

Speaking of tools, it’s amazing the variety of tools that can be found in that workshop. Lots of stuff that’s probably not even made anymore now. Many hammers created by hand by the craftsman and, again, a lot of dust.

These two photos are snaps I took of two photos of the craftsman that were hanging on a piece of furniture. Obviously, the photos show him using chisels.

The last photo, on the other hand, is a “stolen shot” that I took of the craftsman’s son, while he was intent on showing me a sculpture made by his father.

Another thing that fascinated me a lot are the signs I found hanging around the workshop. They are in Italian, so I’ll translate them for you starting from the first:

“No smoking”, of course.
“Keep your workplace in order”, according to his son, order was very important for the craftsman.
“Working hours. Entry. Rest. Exit.”, I suppose this should be the timetable for the employees, but all the fields are empty.

I conclude with some shots taken here and there in the laboratory, while wandering.

What to say? For me it was a very interesting and stimulating journey, I feel that I could have spent days alone in that laboratory and I would have always found something to photograph. In lived things, there is always charm.
